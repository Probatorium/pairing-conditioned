## (a) The question, in one sentence

Where does the observed count of discordant pairs of the King Wen sequence
against the binary order fall within the distribution of that same count over
the family of all sequences that realize the King Wen pairing?

---

## (b) The family, defined before it is built

### The pairing

Radisic (2026), arXiv:2601.07175v3, "Optimal Equivariant Matchings on the
6-Cube: With an Application to the King Wen Sequence", proves that a unique
matching on the vertices of the 6-cube minimizes total Hamming cost among the
matchings admitted by its rule, and gives that matching by a reverse priority
rule: pair each vertex with its reversal, unless the vertex is a palindrome,
in which case pair it with its complement. Call that matching M. M has
thirty-two blocks and every hexagram lies in exactly one block.

This lane takes M as given from that source. It does not reprove it, and it
does not assert it. The claim that the King Wen sequence realizes M is a
claim of that paper, stated there as an isomorphism. Whether the adjacency
pairing of the received King Wen sequence, that is the set of unordered pairs
occupying positions one and two, three and four, and so on to sixty-three and
sixty-four, is literally equal to M as a set partition of the sixty-four
hexagrams, is a precondition of the design below and is checked before
anything else is computed. See the precondition gate in section (d).

### The family

The family F is the set of sequences of all sixty-four hexagrams such that,
for every block position, the two hexagrams occupying that block form a block
of M. A member of F is generated by exactly two free choices:

- a permutation of the thirty-two blocks of M into the thirty-two block
  positions of the sequence, and
- an orientation for each block, that is, which of its two hexagrams occupies
  the earlier of the two positions.

Every such choice yields a distinct member of F, and every member of F arises
from exactly one such choice. F therefore holds

    32! * 2^32 = 1130138339199322632554990773529330319360000000

members, approximately 1.13 * 10^45.

This is the only quantity concerning the family that appears in this document
at signing. It is definitional arithmetic on the declared design, not a
measurement of anything, and section (g) forbids any measurement before this
commit exists.

F is the null. It is not a convenience null. It is exactly the set of
sequences that agree with the King Wen sequence on the one structural fact
that the cited result establishes, and are otherwise unconstrained. The
question of this lane is what the observed count looks like once that fact,
and nothing further, is held fixed.

### Why F is sampled and not enumerated

Enumeration of F is not available at any plausible scale: the cardinal above
exceeds by many orders of magnitude anything that can be traversed. Uniform
sampling from F is available exactly, because both free choices can be
sampled exactly: a uniform permutation of thirty-two objects by Fisher and
Yates, and thirty-two independent fair bits. There is no rejection step, no
approximation, and no burn-in. The Monte Carlo error is therefore the only
error, and it is quantified in section (g).

One component of the statistic does not need sampling at all. Section (c)
decomposes the statistic, and the law of one of its two components is exact
under F. That component is treated exactly, not by simulation.

## (c) The null and the statistic

### The statistic

For a sequence S of the sixty-four hexagrams and a bit convention c, let
T_c(S) be the number of unordered pairs of distinct hexagrams whose relative
order in S disagrees with their relative order in the binary order induced by
c. There are C(64,2) = 2016 such unordered pairs.

### The four bit conventions

A hexagram is six lines. Reading it as a binary integer requires two choices:
which end of the figure carries the most significant bit, and which line type
is one. That is four conventions, and all four are carried through the whole
analysis. They are fixed and named in the analysis code before the observed
value is read, in this declared order: bottom-significant with solid as one;
bottom-significant with solid as zero; top-significant with solid as one;
top-significant with solid as zero.

### Decomposition of the statistic under F

Because the blocks of M occupy adjacent position pairs, every comparison
between two hexagrams in different blocks is settled by the order of those
two blocks alone, and is untouched by orientation. Every comparison within a
block is settled by that block's orientation alone. Hence, for every member
of F,

    T = W + X

where W counts discordances among the thirty-two within-block comparisons and
X counts discordances among the 496 * 4 = 1984 between-block comparisons, and
32 + 1984 = 2016.

Under F the two components have exact and stated laws. W is a sum of
thirty-two independent fair bits, because the two hexagrams of a block have
distinct binary values under every convention and therefore exactly one of
the two orientations is concordant. So W is Binomial with thirty-two trials
and probability one half, exactly, with mean sixteen and variance eight. For
X, each unordered pair of blocks contributes either k or 4 - k according to
which block comes first, where k is a fixed integer of that block pair under
the chosen convention, and each order is equally likely under a uniform
permutation, so each pair of blocks contributes two in expectation and X has
mean 992.

Therefore, under F and for every convention,

    E[T] = 16 + 992 = 1008

exactly. This is an analytic prediction stated in advance. It is used as the
acceptance test of the sampler, and the sampler is not permitted to produce a
single p-value until it reproduces it, together with the exact mean and
variance of W, within declared Monte Carlo tolerance.

The consequence for the reading of results is stated now, before any result
exists, so that it cannot later be presented as a discovery: the value 1008
is not merely the midpoint of the range of possible counts. It is the exact
mean of the null of this lane. An observed count near 1008 is therefore the
generic behaviour of this family and is not, on its own, evidence of anything
at all.

### What is reported

For each convention, and for each of the three quantities T, W and X, the
report gives: the observed value, recomputed here from primary data; the
family mean, standard deviation and empirical support; the percentile of the
observed value in the family; and the declared p-values of section (d). The
percentile is reported for all conventions whatever the outcome, including
when it is unremarkable.

## (d) The refutation criterion

### The precondition gate, which can stop the lane before it starts

Before any test is run, two facts are checked and the outcome is reported
whatever it is:

- P1. The adjacency pairing of the received King Wen sequence equals M as a
  set partition.
- P2. The observed statistic recomputed here from primary data agrees with
  the value reported by the prior lane.

If P1 fails, the family F as defined in (b) is not the family that conditions
on the King Wen pairing, the design of this document does not apply, the
failure is reported as the result of the lane, and no test below is run or
reported. If P2 fails, the discrepancy is reported and this lane's own
recomputed value, not the prior value, is the one carried forward.

### The hypothesis under test

H: the observed count is atypical within F.

### The declared tests

Per distinct convention, four tests, all two sided except where stated:

- A. Location of T. p = P_F(T at least as far from E[T] as observed), by the
  Monte Carlo estimator below.
- B. Location of W, the orientation component. Computed against the exact
  Binomial law, not by simulation.
- C. Location of X, the block order component. This test is not implied by A:
  W and X can each be extreme in opposite directions while T sits at its
  mean, and this test is included precisely to detect that case.
- D. Centrality of T, one sided by construction and declared in this
  direction now. p = P_F(|T - 1008| <= |T_observed - 1008|). This is the test
  that addresses the prior lane's residual, namely whether the observed
  deviation from the exact mean is unusually small.

The tail of A, B and C is two sided. The tail of D is the lower tail of the
absolute deviation. All four tails are fixed here and are not chosen after
seeing any figure.

### The criterion

H is refuted if every one of the declared tests, after the multiplicity
correction of section (e), yields an adjusted p of at least 0.05.

H survives, in the specific and limited sense of not being refuted, if at
least one declared test yields an adjusted p below 0.05, and the report then
names which test, which convention, and which direction.

### Verification that the criterion can fail, performed before signing

The prior lane signed a criterion that no sequence could fail, and that
defect is deposited. So the check is done here explicitly and in both
directions.

Can H be refuted? Yes, and this is the outcome the pre-analysis expects.
Since E[T] = 1008 exactly under F, and the observed count is reported by the
prior lane to sit a few units from that value, test A will place the observed
near the centre of the family unless the family is far more concentrated than
any of the estimates below allow. Refutation is therefore reachable, and is
reachable by the most likely state of the world.

Can H survive? Yes, and the conditions are stated concretely so that the
claim is checkable rather than rhetorical.

- Test D survives refutation if the family spread is large enough that a
  window of eleven integers centred on 1008 holds less than the corrected
  threshold of the family mass. Under an approximately unimodal family law
  that requires a family standard deviation of roughly three hundred and
  fifty or more. A heuristic upper bound on that standard deviation can be
  written down in advance: the between-block component is a linear rank
  statistic on thirty-two blocks whose per pair swing is at most two, which
  bounds its standard deviation by twice the standard deviation of the
  inversion count on thirty-two objects, of order one hundred and
  twenty-five, and the within-block component adds a standard deviation of
  the square root of eight. This bound is heuristic and unproved. It is
  written here so that if the measured spread violates it, the violation is
  visible against a prior commitment rather than absorbed silently.
- Test D also survives refutation if the family law is strongly non unimodal,
  for instance if it has a valley at its own mean. Nothing in the design
  excludes that, and no computation performed before this commit has
  addressed it.
- Tests B and C survive refutation on their own account, and their outcomes
  are genuinely open at signing. W has an exact null with standard deviation
  of the square root of eight, so an orientation pattern that is even
  moderately lopsided across the thirty-two blocks reaches significance. C
  behaves likewise for the ordering of the blocks. Neither is determined by
  the value of T, by the decomposition above.

So: refutation is reachable and expected; survival is reachable through B, C
or a non unimodal family law, and is not expected. This asymmetry is a
declared property of the design and is not a defect concealed. What would be
a defect is a criterion under which refutation could not occur, and by the
first paragraph above, that is not the case here.

### The stated limitation of the design

Because E[T] = 1008 holds exactly under F, test A carries little power
against any alternative that the prior lane's residual could represent. That
is a limitation of the question, not of the execution, and it is recorded now
rather than discovered later. The informative content of this lane, if H is
refuted, is a negative result of a definite kind: that once the pairing is
held fixed, the observed count is an ordinary member of its family and its
distance from 1008 calls for no further explanation. That statement is worth
establishing and this design establishes it. It is not dressed up as more.

## (e) Multiplicity, declared now

Four tests are declared, per distinct convention. Four conventions are
declared. The maximum is therefore sixteen tests.

The conventions are expected to collapse in pairs. Complementing the meaning
of the line types reverses the binary order entirely, which maps T to
2016 - T, W to 32 - W and X to 1984 - X, and leaves the declared statistics
of tests A, B, C and D unchanged up to reflection. This is a prediction, not
an established fact of this repository, and it is verified computationally
before the correction is applied.

The correction rule is fixed now and does not depend on the outcome. Holm's
step down procedure is applied at a family wise level of 0.05 across the set
of distinct tests actually run, where two tests are counted as one if and
only if the invariance above is verified to hold exactly for them. If the
invariance is verified, that set has eight members. If it fails, that set has
sixteen. No other value is permitted, and the choice between eight and
sixteen is settled by a verification that has a determinate answer and not by
inspection of any p-value.

No other test is run. Any further quantity computed in this lane is labelled
exploratory in the report, is excluded from the correction, and cannot
support any claim.

## (f) What will not be claimed

Whatever the figures do, this repository will not claim, suggest, imply or
frame as an open possibility:

- that the King Wen sequence was designed, or was not designed;
- that any intention, purpose or awareness lay behind its ordering;
- that its makers possessed, or lacked, knowledge of binary representation,
  of combinatorics, or of any other mathematical content;
- that a statistical outcome in this family bears on the meaning,
  authenticity, antiquity or authority of the sequence or of any text;
- that a refutation or a survival of H says anything about a person.

The object of study is a permutation of sixty-four objects and a counting
function on it. The results speak about that object and about the family
defined in (b), and about nothing else.

Additionally, and until the background review of this lane is opened and
closed, no claim of novelty, priority or first result of any kind is made
here about anything.

## (g) Sample size and seed, fixed here

- Sample size: 1000000 draws from F per distinct convention. This is fixed.
  It is not increased after seeing a result, and it is not decreased.
- Seed: 20260811. The generator is the PCG64 bit generator, seeded from a
  seed sequence on that integer, with one independent stream spawned per
  convention in the declared convention order of section (c).
- Monte Carlo p-value estimator: p = (1 + number of draws at least as
  extreme) / (1 + number of draws). This never returns zero and is
  conservative. A p reported at its floor is reported as such and never as a
  zero.
- Monte Carlo standard error at this sample size is below 0.0005 for any p in
  the neighbourhood of the thresholds used, which is small against the
  smallest corrected threshold in (e). No result whose verdict would turn on
  Monte Carlo error alone will be declared; if one arises, it is reported as
  indeterminate at this sample size and the sample size is not raised to
  resolve it.
- No adaptive stopping. No sequential looks. One run per convention, with the
  declared seed, reported whatever it yields.
- The sampler must pass the acceptance test of section (c), reproducing the
  analytic mean of T and the exact mean and variance of W within Monte Carlo
  tolerance, before any p-value is read. The observed values are read only
  after that acceptance test passes.
