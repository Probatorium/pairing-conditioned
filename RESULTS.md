# Results

Run 2026-08-11, session three. Every figure below was produced by the code in
`analysis/`, from the seed and the sample size the preregistration fixed, and
the raw output is in `results/`.

Order of operations, which matters and was not chosen afterwards: the
preconditions ran first, then the identity of the family against the deposit
that owns it, then the two validations of the sampler, and only then the
measurement. The analysis code was committed before the measurement was
looked at.

## 1. What the preregistration fixes

Restated from `PREREGISTRATION.md` at the root commit, blob unchanged since
that commit and verified so before anything else was done. Its words are
quoted; the restatement adds nothing.

**The question.** "Where does the observed count of discordant pairs of the
King Wen sequence against the binary order fall within the distribution of
that same count over the family of all sequences that realize the King Wen
pairing?"

**The family.** "The family F is the set of sequences of all sixty-four
hexagrams such that, for every block position, the two hexagrams occupying
that block form a block of M." Generated by "a permutation of the thirty-two
blocks of M into the thirty-two block positions of the sequence" and "an
orientation for each block". Cardinal declared as thirty-two factorial times
two to the thirty-two, that is 1130138339199322632554990773529330319360000000.

**The statistic.** "let T_c(S) be the number of unordered pairs of distinct
hexagrams whose relative order in S disagrees with their relative order in the
binary order induced by c. There are C(64,2) = 2016 such unordered pairs."

**The conventions.** Four, "fixed and named in the analysis code before the
observed value is read, in this declared order: bottom-significant with solid
as one; bottom-significant with solid as zero; top-significant with solid as
one; top-significant with solid as zero."

**The decomposition.** "T = W + X where W counts discordances among the
thirty-two within-block comparisons and X counts discordances among the
496 * 4 = 1984 between-block comparisons". W is "Binomial with thirty-two
trials and probability one half, exactly", X has mean 992, and therefore
"E[T] = 16 + 992 = 1008 exactly".

**The tests and their tails.** "A. Location of T. B. Location of W, the
orientation component. Computed against the exact Binomial law, not by
simulation. C. Location of X, the block order component. D. Centrality of T,
one sided by construction and declared in this direction now. p = P_F(|T -
1008| <= |T_observed - 1008|)." And: "The tail of A, B and C is two sided. The
tail of D is the lower tail of the absolute deviation. All four tails are
fixed here and are not chosen after seeing any figure."

**Sample size and seed.** "Sample size: 1000000 draws from F per distinct
convention. This is fixed." "Seed: 20260811. The generator is the PCG64 bit
generator, seeded from a seed sequence on that integer, with one independent
stream spawned per convention in the declared convention order." Estimator:
"p = (1 + number of draws at least as extreme) / (1 + number of draws)."

**Multiplicity.** "Holm's step down procedure is applied at a family wise
level of 0.05 across the set of distinct tests actually run, where two tests
are counted as one if and only if the invariance above is verified to hold
exactly for them. If the invariance is verified, that set has eight members.
If it fails, that set has sixteen. No other value is permitted."

**The refutation criterion.** "H is refuted if every one of the declared
tests, after the multiplicity correction of section (e), yields an adjusted p
of at least 0.05. H survives, in the specific and limited sense of not being
refuted, if at least one declared test yields an adjusted p below 0.05."

Nothing in the instructions for this session contradicts that document. The
one place where those instructions could have collided with it, the request
that the definitions of W and X be written before running, is what the signed
document already did, and the code follows the signed definitions.

- that deposit prints the cardinal as `1.1301e45`, which is what this lane

That is the same object, defined the same way, with the same cardinal, and it

this lane will describe it as a family this lane introduced. What this lane
does is measure on that family a statistic the prior work did not measure.
That is the whole of the contribution and it is not dressed as more.

## 4. The sampler, validated by two routes

**Route A, the analytic acceptance test the preregistration declares.** On two
hundred thousand draws, in all four conventions: the empirical mean of T is
within 1.37 standard errors of the analytic 1008, the mean of W is within
tolerance of 16 and its variance within tolerance of 8. The tolerance, four
standard errors, was fixed in the code before the run. PASS.

**Route B, against the published implementation of the rung.** The deposit's
own membership predicate, read at its tag with digest recorded and executed
from a scratch file outside this repository, was handed two thousand draws of
this sampler. It accepted every one. This lane's independently written
predicate agreed draw for draw. The deposit's decomposition recovered exactly
the permutation and the orientation vector this sampler had chosen. Four
witnesses, three outside the family and one inside it, were classified
identically by both predicates. PASS.

The two routes agree. A reimplementation inherits the verifications of the
original, and this is that inheritance performed rather than promised.

### The decomposition is where the open tests were

Recorded as decision four required of this document. The location test on T
had little power by construction, because the family mean is the balance
point exactly. The tests that could have gone either way on their own account
were B and C, on the orientation component and on the block order component,

mean. They were run, they are reported above, and neither is extreme: the
orientation component sits at the sixty-third percentile of an exactly known

That is the answer to the part of the question that was genuinely open, and
it is a null answer.

## 6. The verdict against the signed criterion

The criterion, quoted from preregistration section (d):

> "H is refuted if every one of the declared tests, after the multiplicity
> correction of section (e), yields an adjusted p of at least 0.05."

Every one of the eight declared tests yields an adjusted p of at least 0.05.
The smallest adjusted p is 0.426112.

**The first branch of the criterion is the one that was met. H is refuted.**

H was: "the observed count is atypical within F." It is not. The observed
count sits at the fifty-second percentile of its family, five units from a
mean of 1008 whose family has a standard deviation of 80.450.

The criterion did not have to come out this way and the document said in
advance which way it expected. From section (d): "Can H be refuted? Yes, and
this is the outcome the pre-analysis expects." And it wrote down what would
have had to be true for the other branch: "that requires a family standard
deviation of roughly three hundred and fifty or more", against a heuristic
upper bound it recorded in advance as "of order one hundred and twenty-five".

The measured family standard deviation is 80.450. The advance bound held. The
advance expectation was met.

**The verdict does not turn on the multiplicity count.** The smallest p
across all sixteen computed tests is 0.053264, which is already at or above
0.05 with no correction at all. Correcting over one, four, eight or sixteen
tests gives the same verdict. This is stated because a refutation that
depended on how many tests were counted would be a weak refutation.

### What this result says, and what it does not

It says that once the pairing is held fixed, the observed count is an
ordinary member of its family, and that the distance of five between the
observed count and the balance point calls for no explanation beyond the
pairing itself. The residual the prior lane left open is accounted for by
the family it sits in.

It does not say the pairing itself is unremarkable. The family conditions on
the pairing, so the pairing is exactly the thing this measurement cannot see.
Whether the pairing is remarkable is the question of the theorem this lane
cites and does not reprove.

## 7. Two things the signed document did not fix

Declared, not reinterpreted. Both are in `DEFECTS.md`.

**The acceptance tolerance was not a number.** Section (c) required the
sampler to reproduce the analytic mean "within declared Monte Carlo
tolerance" and section (g) fixed a tolerance for p-values but not for that
test. Four standard errors was fixed in the code before the run. The largest
deviation observed was 1.37 standard errors, so no plausible choice of
tolerance would have changed the outcome.

**Which member of a collapsed pair supplies the p.** Section (e) says two
tests count as one under the verified invariance, but not which of the two
independently sampled estimates is carried forward. The smaller of the two
was taken, that being the choice least favourable to the outcome the document
expected. The two members differ by less than 0.0007 in every case, which is
Monte Carlo noise.

**And one finding the multiplicity rule did not anticipate.** Reversing the
reading swaps the two members of every block that is a reversal pair, so every
block keeps its value set, so the between-block table is unchanged and with it
the entire law of X. The first and third conventions therefore share a family
law, as do the second and fourth, and here they share their observed values
too, because exactly 14 of the 28 reversal blocks are discordant as received.
The genuine number of distinct tests is four, not eight.

The signed rule permits collapsing only under the complement invariance it
named, and "no other value is permitted". So the correction was run over
eight, as the rule requires, and this finding is reported and not acted on.
Acting on it would have made the correction weaker, which would have made
refutation easier, which is the direction of the expected outcome. The
verdict is the same over four and over eight.
